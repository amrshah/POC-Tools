Management conclusion

Price the implementation and the five-year service obligation separately.

The project needs a temporary multidisciplinary delivery team for discovery, design, build, migration, accessibility, testing and launch. After go-live, that team should reduce to a small operational function for application support, monitoring, security maintenance, incident response and disaster-recovery readiness. The model below keeps these staffing pools separate so the five-year price is not inflated by carrying the full implementation team into operations—or understated by ignoring ongoing obligations.

Project execution roadmap

A six-month implementation plan with overlapping workstreams, followed by 60 months of managed operations (months 7–66). Phase durations are planning windows, not additive effort estimates.

PHASE 01 · WEEKS 1–3

Mobilization & discovery

Kick-off, stakeholder interviews, current-site audit, requirements, analytics, integrations register, risks and acceptance criteria.

3 weeks
PHASE 02 · WEEKS 2–6

Content inventory & information architecture

Count pages, PDFs and page counts, Office files, forms and media; define taxonomy, navigation, content types, migration rules and archive priorities.

5 weeks · overlaps discovery
PHASE 03 · WEEKS 4–9

UX, design & accessibility planning

Resident/staff journeys, responsive design system, page templates, WCAG 2.1 AA criteria and accessible-document approach.

6 weeks
PHASE 04 · WEEKS 7–17

CMS platform build & integrations

CMS setup, templates, permissions, search, forms, integrations, hosting, CI/CD, security controls, backups and monitoring.

11 weeks
PHASE 05 · WEEKS 10–21

Migration & remediation

Content mapping, data entry/import, PDF and document triage, accessibility remediation, link checks, deduplication and sample-based reconciliation.

12 weeks · archive-dependent
PHASE 06 · WEEKS 18–24

QA, UAT, training & launch

Functional, browser, performance, security and accessibility testing; acceptance, editor training, launch rehearsal, go-live and hypercare.

7 weeks
PHASE 07 · MONTHS 7–66

Managed operations

Monitoring, patching, backup verification, recovery testing, incident response, CMS support, security maintenance, reporting and controlled change requests.

A reduced support allocation may fail the SLA, so use lean scenarios for sensitivity—not as proof that contractual coverage can be met.

Historical incumbent vendor spend — RFP reference

Addendum 2, Question 83 reports approximately $8,302.85 per year for the District's prior website arrangement (about $692/month, or approximately $41,514 over five years if simply annualized). This is historical context only—not an approved budget, price ceiling or directly comparable quote for this procurement. The new solicitation has a much broader specified scope, including implementation, migration, accessibility, security, hosting and five years of managed services. Question 2 indicates no established budget was provided. Verify the official addendum wording before external use.

Do not confuse the two $8K figures: the ~$8.3K is reported historical annual vendor spend. A previous internal work breakdown also showed an illustrative $8,000 migration line (500 hours × $16/hour). They are unrelated numbers.

Major cost and delivery risks

  • Content inventory: page count, PDFs and their page counts, Office documents, forms, images, duplicates and broken links.
  • Accessibility remediation: current web pages and archived documents may require manual review and remediation.
  • Security assurance: clarify whether ISO 27001 / SOC 2 obligations apply to the prime, hosting provider or both.
  • Support SLA: business-hours helpdesk is not the same as 24×7 critical incident response.
  • Resilience: one low-cost VPS is not automatically adequate for 99.9% availability and recovery obligations.
  • US commercial overhead: insurance, travel, legal review, payment, tax and specialist subcontractors may be outside this model.

Assumptions to validate before any bid

  • Actual site and archive inventory, including file counts and PDF page counts.
  • Migration responsibility: automated import, manual data entry, document remediation and approvals.
  • Required integrations, forms, resident requests and third-party licensing.
  • Support hours, response times, escalation, on-call and included enhancement limits.
  • Backup frequency, RPO/RTO, restore tests, redundancy and hosting region.
  • Prime contractor eligibility, required corporate certifications and insurance.
Model limitations: This is an internal planning mockup, not a fixed-price quotation. Hourly rates are assumed internal loaded delivery costs. Confirm rates, specialist quotes, hosting architecture, taxes, insurance, travel, licensing and all RFP compliance obligations before using a final price.
